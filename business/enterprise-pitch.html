Enterprise & Public Sector

Executive Pitch Deck Outline

Audience & Framing

Built for a 20–25 minute boardroom slot with C-level executives, CEOs, and senior managers — including public-sector audiences like NHS Trust boards. The throughline across every slide: certified employees are an operational and risk-management lever, not a training-budget line item. Numbers and case studies should be pressure-tested against the organization before delivery; placeholders below are marked accordingly.

Slide-by-Slide Outline

Slide 1

Title

"Certified AI Capability: A Board-Level Lever for Operational Performance." Organization logo alongside Cambridge AI, presenter name and title, date.

Slide 2

The Business Problem, Not the Technology Problem

Frame AI adoption as a workforce-capability gap, not a tooling decision. For NHS/public-sector audiences: reference the specific pressure points already on the board's agenda — staff capacity, waiting-list pressure, administrative burden — rather than opening with "AI trends."

Slide 3

Why Certification, Not Just Training

Distinguish verified competency (certification, assessed capstone work) from attendance-based training. The board-level risk being managed: staff "using AI" without demonstrated, auditable competency.

Slide 4

Operational Efficiency Impact

Present time-saved or throughput metrics from certified vs. non-certified staff (use organization-specific pilot data where available; otherwise clearly label as an industry-benchmark estimate). For NHS: framed as clinician/administrative time returned to patient care.

Slide 5

Patient & Customer Perception

Evidence or projection that staff confidently and correctly using AI tools improves the experience of the person on the other side of the interaction — patient trust and safety perception for NHS; customer satisfaction/NPS for commercial enterprise.

Slide 6

Risk & Governance

How certification supports the organization's existing governance and compliance posture — an auditable record of who is trained, to what standard, and when it was last verified. Critical for NHS information governance and clinical safety sign-off.

Slide 7

The Delivery Model

How the program actually runs: cohort sizing, on-site or remote delivery, and integration with existing L&D calendars. Cross-reference the Pricing & Scheduling Model for the operational detail without putting numbers on this slide.

Slide 8

Proof Points

Case study or pilot cohort results: completion rate, certification pass rate, and one direct quote from a participating manager or clinician lead. Keep to one slide, one story, done well.

Slide 9

Investment & ROI Framing

Present per-seat investment against the operational-efficiency and risk-reduction case made on Slides 4–6, not as a standalone cost. Use the formula from the Pricing & Scheduling Model to generate the specific figure for this organization's likely cohort size.

Slide 10

Proposed Pilot

A single, low-risk, time-boxed pilot: one department, one cohort, one term, with clearly agreed success metrics defined jointly with the board before it starts.

Slide 11

Decision & Next Steps

Explicit ask: sponsor name, budget holder sign-off, and proposed pilot start date. Close with a single next action (e.g. "confirm pilot department by [date]"), not an open-ended "let us know."

Delivery Notes

  • Keep text density low — this is a spoken deck, not a leave-behind document. Prepare a separate one-page leave-behind summarizing Slides 2, 4, 6, and 9.
  • For NHS and public-sector boards specifically, have information governance and clinical safety answers ready before the meeting — this is almost always the first follow-up question, not an objection to be handled reactively.
  • Never present unverified statistics as organization-specific results; label industry benchmarks clearly and offer a pilot as the way to generate the organization's own numbers.
